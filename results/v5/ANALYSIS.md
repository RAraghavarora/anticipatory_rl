# v5 analysis: the structural findings behind the numbers

`RESULTS.md` holds the costs. This file holds the measurements that explain *why* they come
out that way — the derived quantities, the diagnoses, and the arithmetic. Every number here
was measured, not estimated; the reproduction command is given for each.

Hand-written. Not regenerated by `build_v5_results.py`.

---

## 1. Cost model — action penalties dominate movement

Movement is `25 x dijkstra_distance`. PDDL action costs (and the reported `paper2` metric,
which **includes** them — verify via `env.paper2_fixed_costs`):

| action | cost | in movement-hops |
|---|---|---|
| `fill` | **1000** | 40 |
| `pour`, `wash` | 200 | 8 |
| `pick`, `place` | 100 | 4 |
| `refill_water` | **50** | 2 |
| `make-coffee`, `drain` | 50 | 2 |

The jar's value is the **`fill`(1000) -> `refill_water`(50) asymmetry**, not travel. Per water
task it saves ~1350 (`make_coffee`, fountain 8 hops from coffeemachine) or ~1050
(`serve_water`, fountain 2 hops from servingtable).

**Any arithmetic that prices only movement is wrong by ~3.3x.** That error produced two
incorrect design recommendations during this work.

Both `serve_water` and `make_coffee` consume water, so the jar amortises over their union —
**54% of tasks**, not the 32% `make_coffee` share.

## 2. Why v4 produced no K=3 advantage (and why the jar starts empty)

v4 made two changes that cancelled:

| domain | jar start | investment | break-even | minimum useful K |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| level_3 | shelf (2 hops), **empty** | ~250 + `fill` 1000 | ~1.3 water tasks | **2** |
| v4 | pantry (35 hops), **pre-filled** | 1,950 | ~1.44 | **2** |
| **v5** | pantry (35 hops), **empty** | ~3,000 | **~2.5** | **3** |

Moving the jar 33 hops further raised the fetch cost by 1,650, but pre-filling it removed the
1,000-cost `fill` from the investment *and* made every use save a full `fill` rather than only
repeat ones. Net movement in break-even: 1.3 -> 1.44. Both saturate at K=2, which is exactly
where the one-task baseline sits — hence no measurable advantage.

Confirmed with an **optimal** planner, so it is not a search artifact: K=2 `astar(hmax())`
fetches the jar at window 5 on v4, and K=3 fetches the same jar at the same window.

**Deleting `filled_with: water` is what creates the headroom.** Distance alone does not.

## 3. A one-task expectation cannot justify the investment — exact, not estimated

`C_AP(s) = sum_tau P(tau) * optimal_plan_cost(s, tau)` computed **exactly with Fast Downward**
over all 48 enumerated tasks. This bounds *any* method whose value signal is a one-task
expectation, including a perfectly-fitted GNN.

    PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/restaurant/probe_cap_jar.py \
        --config-path configs/restaurant/toy_level_5.yaml --place-at servingtable

| state | C_AP |
|---|---|
| jar at pantry, empty (initial) | **785.3** |
| jar filled, at servingtable | **535.0** |
| jar filled, at coffeemachine | **518.4** |

- credit a one-task expectation can offer: **250.3**
- cost of the investment: **3,000**
- **coverage: 8.3%. Shortfall: 12x.**

The GNN's selection rule is `score = prefix_cost + gamma * C_AP(terminal)`, minimised
(`scripts/gnn/eval_sequence.py:287`), with `--gamma` defaulting to **1.0** — already its
maximum useful value. So no hyperparameter closes the gap:

    myopic candidate : 0    + 1.0 * 785.3 =  785.3
    jar candidate    : 3000 + 1.0 * 535.0 = 3535.0   -> +2749.7 worse, rejected

**Neither placement dominates** (535.0 vs 518.4, a 3% difference): the jar helps
`serve_water` at the servingtable and `make_coffee` at the coffeemachine, and no single
location serves both. This is why the K=3 clairvoyant plans *relocate* the jar mid-sequence —
a decision no one-task method can represent.

## 4. The baseline cannot propose the investment either (spatial bound)

`_generate_focused_augmentations` (`scripts/gnn/eval_sequence.py:145`) bounds candidate
preparations to the myopic plan's path **plus one hop** (`_dijkstra_distance <= 1.0`).

| domain | jar location | inside the bounded region? |
|---|---|---|
| level_3 | `shelf` | **yes** — one hop from `countertop`, where objects reset |
| v5 | `pantry` | **no** — `one-hop(pantry) == ['pantry']` |

So `jar_0` never enters `relevant_objects`, and no clause mentioning it is emitted, except for
the ~0.8% of tasks that are `pick_place(jar_0, .)`.

**Measured consequence:** v5 yields **4.03 augmented candidates per state** against level_3's
**4.86** — 22% fewer. This survived the encoder fix (adding `pantry` to `ROOM_ASSIGNMENTS`),
confirming it is a property of the Dijkstra-bounded candidate generator, not of the encoding.

A second, independent limit: each candidate carries exactly **one** goal clause, so it can
request *"jar at coffeemachine"* or *"jar filled"* but never both — and on v5 the jar starts
empty, so it needs both.

## 5. The steelman: candidate handed over, still declined

`--unbounded-jar-augmentation` (commits `e15dcf6`, `c44cfe1`, `d6e9a25`, `720d038`) removes
both limits in section 4: it emits an unconditional **conjunctive** clause (filled AND
relocated), and the training set was regenerated to contain those states so `C_AP` for them is
learned rather than extrapolated.

| | count |
|---|---|
| jar candidates **offered** | **741** |
| successfully planned | 712 |
| **scored** by the GNN | 712 |
| failed (planner timeout) | 29 (3.9%) |
| **accepted** | **1** |

Total cost 654,275 vs the unmodified baseline's 4-seed mean of 653,738 — a gap of 537, which
is INSIDE that arm's own seed spread (+/-567). Not merely no improvement: statistically
indistinguishable. (The original comparison used seed 42's 650,775, a single draw.)

The decline is therefore **not** a candidate-generation failure: the candidate was present,
solved, and evaluated 712 times. It is the value horizon, exactly as section 3 predicts.

**Validity gate:** a decline is only meaningful when `solved == scored` and failures are few.
Here 712/741 were solved and scored; even if all 29 timeouts would have been accepted, that is
4%. Not timeout-driven. (This gate exists because an earlier K-comparison in this project was
invalidated by exactly that confound — see section 8.)

## 6. Counterfactual augmentation collapses across domains

The GNN's central contribution, measured on both domains with the same architecture, training
protocol, encoder, and comparable sample counts:

All four cells are now 4-seed means (seeds 0/4/8/16); the earlier version of this table used
single seed-42 draws, which flattered the baseline in both domains.

| domain | faithful | augmented | augmentation gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| level_3 (theirs) | 670,669 +/- 5,968 | 472,594 +/- 1,852 | **-29.5%** |
| v5 (long-horizon) | 671,238 +/- 2,970 | 653,738 +/- 567 | **-2.6%** |

**A factor of ten.** And on v5 the faithful arm is only -0.81% against myopic, so without
augmentation the method does essentially nothing.

**This is not a fitting failure.** The GNN reaches validation L1 **49.76** on v5 against
**50.3** on level_3 — statistically identical accuracy. It approximates `C_AP` on v5 exactly as
well as on the domain it was designed for.

## 7. Cross-domain comparison (the fairness control)

level_3 totals must be normalised by seed count — `results/canonical_planner/planner/run_summary.csv`
has `n=40` for the DQN arms (4 seeds x 10 sequences):

| | level_3 (theirs) | v5 (long-horizon) |
|---|---|---|
| GNN published (4 seeds) | 472,594 +/- 1,852 | 653,738 +/- 567 |
| ours | 457,138 | 498,644 +/- 69,214 |
| **ours vs GNN** | **-3.3%** | **-23.7%** |
| K=2 optimal (one-task ceiling) | 404,625 | 614,575 |
| **ours vs that ceiling** | **+13.0%** | **-18.9%** |

The last two rows are the sharpest form of the claim, and they are immune to the objections
the cost comparison invites: the K=2 bound is an EXACT planner (astar(hmax()), 0/500 windows
at cap in both domains) computed under the same P(tau) and the same sequences, so it cannot be
contaminated by tuning or by baseline implementation quality. On their domain we do not exceed
what one-task lookahead can achieve; on the long-horizon variant we go 18.9% past it.

Statistically tied on their domain, decisively better on the long-horizon one. This is what
makes v5 a controlled probe rather than an arena built for a win.

## 8. Planner-budget confound (why timeout rates are reported everywhere)

`seq-sat-lama-2011` is anytime: larger K -> larger search space -> more windows hit the budget
-> worse plans. On v4 at 120s, K=3 timed out on 62% of windows vs K=2's 38%, inflating K=3's
apparent deficit from 3.86% to 7.85%. **Never compare K arms on raw cost without the
timeout rate.** Use `scripts/restaurant/compare_k_arms.py`, which splits on convergence.

Live example in this archive: **seq-01's K=4 (60,650) is worse than its K=3 (42,275)** despite
deeper lookahead, with 28/50 windows at the cap. That is the confound, not a finding about
lookahead. Same for seq-05.

Exact search is not an escape at K>=3: `astar(hmax())` on a K=3 sequence dies at the first
window containing three water tasks (960s, no plan) — i.e. exactly where the interesting
decisions are. All five `seq-opt-*` aliases reject this domain's axioms
(4 `:derived` predicates including `current-task-satisfied`); `astar(hmax())` is the usable
optimal search, and it is feasible only at K<=2.

## 9. Discount factor: horizon vs stability

The jar never depletes, so its payoff is a geometric tail over every remaining water task
(~23 of them), not just the 2.5 needed to break even. In RL units (investment ~30.5, saving
~12/water task, water tasks every ~12.8 primitive steps):

| gamma | credit over full tail | beats 30.5 investment? |
|---|---|---|
| 0.95 | 13.0 | no |
| 0.97 | 25.2 | no |
| 0.98 | 40.6 | yes, x1.3 |
| 0.99 | 83.0 | yes, x2.7 |

Measured outcome, matching the direction of that arithmetic — and revealing the tradeoff:

| gamma | jar share (policy rollouts) | task success | stable seeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.97 | 27-33% | 0.806-0.847 | **4/4** |
| 0.98 | 60-68% | 0.467-0.750 | 2/3 |
| 0.99 | 9-62% | 0.151-0.533 | 1/2 |

Jar usage rises with gamma, task success falls, and training stability collapses (worst
`max|Q|`: 632 -> 7.0e7 -> 1.9e10). **gamma=0.97 is the operating point**: 4/4 stable and, at
guided-evaluation time, as good as 0.98.

**Behavioural jar usage does not predict guided performance.** gamma=0.97 uses the jar in only
27-33% of its own rollouts yet guides the planner as well as gamma=0.98's 60-68% — because the
planner needs `V_AP` to *rank* jar states correctly, not the policy to *execute* the fetch.

`R*/Q` is the mechanism: R*=74.01 against mean Q of 132 (gamma=0.97) vs ~300 (gamma=0.99), so
completing a task is worth 56% of state value at 0.97 but only 25% at 0.99. At 0.99 the value
function rewards holding a good position over finishing tasks — visible as `clear_containers`
success collapsing to 0.00 and `pick_place` to 0.11.

## 11. Sequence properties

Water tasks (`serve_water` + `make_coffee`) are 54% of the stream. Windows containing enough
water tasks to justify the investment, across all 10 sequences:

| window | >=2 water tasks | >=3 water tasks |
|---|---|---|
| K=2 (2 tasks) | 35% | **0% — combinatorially impossible** |
| K=3 (3 tasks) | 58% | 23% |

A K=2 window holds two tasks, so it can *never* contain three water tasks, under any
distribution. That is a hard guarantee, not a statistical one.

**Sequences 00 and 02 contain a `pick_place(jar_0, .)` task**, so the task stream itself
delivers the investment. Retained deliberately as a control — excluding them would condition
the eval set on the very mechanism under test. Their effect is dose-dependent:

| seq | jar delivered | to | water tasks remaining after | K=2 optimal gain over myopic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | task 30 | `shelf` (not a consumer) | 5 | -7.1% |
| 02 | task 14 | `fountain` (2 hops from servingtable) | 24 | **-33.0%** |

Other sequences: -3% to -7%. The advantage scales with how much amortisation remains when the
jar arrives — mechanistic evidence rather than a binary control.

`iid-eval-seq-04` has **no** 3-water window anywhere in 50 tasks, so the mechanism cannot fire
there regardless of method.

## 12. Reproduction traps

- **Object naming.** The jar is `jar_0`, matching `experiments/sequences/*.json`, which are
  generated from `toy_level_3.yaml` by `scripts/restaurant/generate_eval_sequences.py`
  (hard-coded path). It was briefly `jar_1` in the pantry config, which made seq-00 and seq-02
  unrunnable. **Always match `/jar/` generically** — hard-coding an index caused three separate
  wrong conclusions during this work.
- **`eval_sequence.py --config-path` defaults to `toy_level_3.yaml`.** A v5-trained GNN loads
  cleanly on the 6-location level_3 env (same `in_dim`, same hidden), so omitting the flag
  silently evaluates the wrong domain with no error.
- **`_jar_ready` in `toy_bellman_novelty_planner.py:237` hard-codes `location ==
  "coffeemachine"`**, so `jar_ready_task_count` reads 0 while the planner is parking the jar at
  the servingtable. Do not trust that field on v5; count jar actions in the trace instead.
- **`--scheduler-step` counts batches, not epochs.** A larger dataset therefore gets more lr
  halvings for the same `--epochs`, and trains less. Matching schedules across datasets
  improved the steelman's val L1 from 67.78 to 56.40.
- **One planner-artifact label** (`v_ap` 50,820 vs its shard's next-highest 2,102) was excluded
  from the steelman training set under a rule applied identically to all three datasets
  (drop `v_ap > 5 x p99.9`); it is a no-op on `aug` and `faithful`.
